================================================================================
                       STATISTICAL INFERENCE COMPREHENSIVE REPORT               
             Applied Hypothesis Testing & Organizational Decision Insights       
                       Food Price Volatility Project (IT3081)                   
================================================================================

This report presents formal hypothesis testing for food price volatility across Sri Lankan
retail markets, addressing comparison of means, proportions, variances, ANOVA, and predictive models.
Dataset: 4629 validated monthly observations across 5 commodities.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 1: COMPARISON OF MEANS (Welch's Two-Sample t-test)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hypotheses:
    Null (H0)       : H0: mu_tomatoes - mu_rice = 0
    Alternative (H1): H1: mu_tomatoes - mu_rice != 0

  Results:
    Test Statistic  : 29.7276
    Degrees of Freedom: 905.7
    p-value         : 4.5038e-136
    Effect Size     : 1.4392 (Cohen's d)

  Conclusion:
    Significant mean difference (diff=37.22%, t=29.73, p<0.001)

  Practical Organizational Implications:
    Use long-term fixed contracts for staples; use dynamic pricing collars and 25% budget buffers for perishables.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 2: COMPARISON OF PROPORTIONS (Two-Sample Test of Proportions (Chi-Square))
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hypotheses:
    Null (H0)       : H0: p_tomatoes - p_rice = 0
    Alternative (H1): H1: p_tomatoes - p_rice != 0

  Results:
    Test Statistic  : 1222.0205
    Degrees of Freedom: 1
    p-value         : 9.9923e-268
    Effect Size     : 35.0841 (Relative Risk (RR))

  Conclusion:
    Significant difference in shock probability (RR=35.08, X2=1222.0, p<0.001)

  Practical Organizational Implications:
    Maintain physical buffer stocks only for low-shock staples; use inflation-indexed cash vouchers for produce.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 3: COMPARISON OF VARIANCES (Fligner-Killeen & Two-Sample F-test)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hypotheses:
    Null (H0)       : H0: sigma_1^2 = ... = sigma_5^2
    Alternative (H1): H1: Variances differ across commodities

  Results:
    Test Statistic  : 2359.5969
    Degrees of Freedom: 4
    p-value         : 0.0000e+00
    Effect Size     : 185.9528 (Variance Ratio (Tomatoes / Rice))

  Conclusion:
    Severe variance heteroscedasticity detected (Fligner X2=2359.6, F-ratio=185.95, p<0.001)

  Practical Organizational Implications:
    Establish commodity-specific Value-at-Risk reserves; reject uniform volatility assumptions across food basket.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 4: ANOVA (One-Way ANOVA & Post-Hoc Tukey HSD)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hypotheses:
    Null (H0)       : H0: mu_1 = mu_2 = ... = mu_5
    Alternative (H1): H1: At least one group mean differs

  Results:
    Test Statistic  : 666.7817
    Degrees of Freedom: 4
    p-value         : 0.0000e+00
    Effect Size     : 0.3658 (Eta-squared (eta^2))

  Conclusion:
    Decisive commodity effect on mean instability (F=666.8, eta2=0.366, p<0.001)

  Practical Organizational Implications:
    Establish three-tier supply procurement: weekly agile sourcing for Tier 1 vs annual volume contracts for Tier 3.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 5: NON-PARAMETRIC GROUP COMPARISON (Kruskal-Wallis Rank Sum Test)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hypotheses:
    Null (H0)       : H0: Median distributions identical across foods
    Alternative (H1): H1: At least one commodity distribution differs

  Results:
    Test Statistic  : 2546.3563
    Degrees of Freedom: 4
    p-value         : 0.0000e+00
    Effect Size     : 0.5498 (Eta-squared (rank-based))

  Conclusion:
    Significant food effect on price instability ranks (p<0.001)

  Practical Organizational Implications:
    Institutional food budgets must structure category-specific purchasing guidelines.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 6: MIXED-EFFECTS MODEL COMPARISON (Incremental F-test (Food vs Regional Market Model))
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hypotheses:
    Null (H0)       : H0: Regional markets contribute zero additional variance
    Alternative (H1): H1: Regional markets explain significant variance

  Results:
    Test Statistic  : 0.3773
    Degrees of Freedom: 8
    p-value         : 9.3312e-01
    Effect Size     : 0.3658 (Marginal R^2)

  Conclusion:
    No significant market effect after food control

  Practical Organizational Implications:
    Centralize procurement nationally; regional price variation is secondary to national commodity shocks.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
TEST 7: LOGISTIC VOLATILITY PREDICTION (Logistic Regression & LRT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hypotheses:
    Null (H0)       : H0: Rolling volatility does not predict price shocks (OR = 1)
    Alternative (H1): H1: Rolling volatility predicts price shocks (OR != 1)

  Results:
    Test Statistic  : 9.1883
    Degrees of Freedom: 1
    p-value         : 2.4357e-03
    Effect Size     : 0.004 (Nagelkerke pseudo-R^2)

  Conclusion:
    Statistically significant predictor (OR=0.992, p<0.01)

  Practical Organizational Implications:
    Calibrate early-warning alert thresholds primarily using commodity risk tiers rather than trailing volatility.

================================================================================
SUMMARY OF METHODOLOGICAL LIMITATIONS
================================================================================
1. Temporal Autocorrelation: While clustered mixed models account for market-level
   random intercepts, residual time series dependence across months may still
   slightly compress standard errors.
2. Extreme Shock Events: Macroeconomic currency devaluations in 2022 produced
   structural price breaks; robust non-parametric tests (Fligner-Killeen, Kruskal-Wallis)
   and Welch-adjusted tests were incorporated to ensure validity under heteroscedasticity.
